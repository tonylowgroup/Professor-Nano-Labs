Three Qubits: Toffoli Gates and Teleportation

With three qubits, a gate is a rewiring of an eight-row truth table. Build reversible logic from Toffoli and Fredkin gates and watch the rows move, then send a qubit's state from Alice to Bob, one step at a time, with a shared entangled pair and two ordinary bits.

Three qubits, eight rows

Three qubits have eight basis states, from \(|000\rangle\) to \(|111\rangle\), with qubit 1 written first. A state is a list of eight complex amplitudes, one per row of a truth table. The size of each amplitude gives the chance of reading that row's three bits; its phase is drawn here as a colour. A gate is an \(8\times8\) unitary matrix: it moves amplitude between rows and can change signs, but it never loses anything, so every gate can be undone.

$$|\psi\rangle=\sum_{a,b,c\,\in\,\{0,1\}}c_{abc}\,|abc\rangle,\qquad \sum_{abc}|c_{abc}|^2=1,\qquad |\psi\rangle\to U|\psi\rangle,\quad U^\dagger U=I$$

The Bloch sphere lab covers one and two qubits; this lab adds the third.

A gate rewires the truth table.

CNOT, Toffoli and Fredkin only reorder rows. On plain bit strings they act exactly like classical logic, but they keep their inputs, so nothing is thrown away. H splits each row in two and gives one branch a minus sign; Z and CCZ change only signs. Every gate here undoes itself. Build a circuit, step through it, and watch the rows move.

$$\text{CNOT}:\ |a,b\rangle\to|a,\,b\oplus a\rangle,\qquad \text{Toffoli}:\ |a,b,c\rangle\to|a,\,b,\,c\oplus ab\rangle,\qquad \text{Fredkin}:\ |1,b,c\rangle\to|1,c,b\rangle$$

Move a qubit with one shared pair and two bits.

Alice has an unknown state \(|\psi\rangle=\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle\) on qubit 1. She and Bob share an entangled pair: qubit 2 stays with Alice, qubit 3 is Bob's. After two gates, the state splits into four groups, one for each result Alice can get when she measures her two qubits. In every group, Bob's qubit already holds \(|\psi\rangle\), flipped in a known way. Alice's two-bit result tells Bob which flip to undo; until it arrives, his qubit is a fair coin.

$$\big(H_1\,\mathrm{CNOT}_{1\to2}\big)\,|\psi\rangle_1|\Phi^+\rangle_{23}=\frac12\sum_{m_1,m_2}|m_1m_2\rangle\otimes X^{m_2}Z^{m_1}|\psi\rangle,\qquad |\Phi^+\rangle=\frac{|00\rangle+|11\rangle}{\sqrt2}$$

      Step 2 · Predict, then test

      Predict first

      Load each example, make a prediction, then step through it to check.

      Question 1 · Truth tables

      A Toffoli gate with qubit 3 as its target acts on \(|110\rangle\). What comes out? Which of the eight rows does this gate ever change?

      Show answer

      \(|111\rangle\). The Toffoli flips qubit 3 only when qubits 1 and 2 are both 1, so it swaps rows \(|110\rangle\) and \(|111\rangle\) and leaves the other six alone. With qubit 3 starting at 0 it computes AND, but, unlike a classical AND gate, it keeps both inputs.

      Question 2 · Truth tables

      A Toffoli onto qubit 3, then CNOT 1→2, acting on \(|a\,b\,0\rangle\). What do the three output bits hold? Try \(a=b=1\).

      Show answer

      \(|a,\ a\oplus b,\ ab\rangle\): qubit 2 holds the sum bit and qubit 3 the carry. For \(1+1\) the output is \(|101\rangle\): sum 0, carry 1, which is 10 in binary. This is a half adder, built only from reversible gates.

      Question 3 · Truth tables

      Apply the same Toffoli twice. What is the result, for every input? Why can a classical AND gate not do this?

      Show answer

      Every input comes back unchanged: the second Toffoli swaps the two rows back. Each row of a reversible gate goes to exactly one row and comes from exactly one, so the input can always be recovered. A classical AND has two inputs and one output: from an output of 0 you cannot tell whether the inputs were 00, 01 or 10, so it cannot be undone. Quantum gates are unitary and must be reversible.

      Question 4 · Truth tables

      Put qubits 1 and 2 into superposition with H, then apply the Toffoli. How many rows carry weight? If you measure, do you get the whole AND truth table?

      Show answer

      Four rows, each with amplitude ½: \(|000\rangle,|010\rangle,|100\rangle,|111\rangle\). The gate acted on all four inputs at once. But one measurement gives just one row, chosen at random with chance ¼ each, so you read one line of the table, not all of it. Qubit 3 is now entangled with the others: on its own its arrow has length ½.

      Question 5 · Truth tables

      A CNOT copies a bit: \(|a\,0\rangle\to|a\,a\rangle\). Put qubit 1 in \(|+\rangle\) first. Do you get two copies of \(|+\rangle\)?

      Show answer

      No. You get \((|000\rangle+|110\rangle)/\sqrt2\), a Bell pair on qubits 1 and 2, and both of their arrows shrink to zero. Two copies, \(|{+}{+}\rangle\), would spread over four rows, \(|000\rangle,|010\rangle,|100\rangle,|110\rangle\). A CNOT copies 0 and 1 but not their superposition, and no gate can: this is the no-cloning theorem.

      Question 6 · Teleportation

      Just before Alice measures, what is the chance of each of her four results? Does it depend on \(|\psi\rangle\)?

      Show answer

      Exactly ¼ each, for every message. Her two bits carry no information about \(\alpha\) or \(\beta\), so she can send them over an open line, and an eavesdropper learns nothing about \(|\psi\rangle\). Teleport 1000 times with different messages: the four bars stay level.

      Question 7 · Teleportation

      Alice has measured, but her two bits have not reached Bob. How often would his qubit pass a check against \(|\psi\rangle\)? And what is left on Alice's side?

      Show answer

      Half the time, the same as a random guess: Bob holds one of four flipped versions of \(|\psi\rangle\), with equal chances, and on average his arrow sits at the centre. So nothing reaches Bob faster than the two classical bits. Alice is left with plain bits, \(|m_1m_2\rangle\), and no trace of \(|\psi\rangle\): the state was moved, not copied, as the no-cloning theorem requires.

      Question 8 · Teleportation

      Mix the shared pair with noise, \(v=0.5\). How faithful is Bob's qubit after the correction? Below what \(v\) could Alice do as well by just measuring her qubit and phoning Bob the result?

      Show answer

      The fidelity is \((1+v)/2=75\%\) for any message, and Bob's arrow shrinks to length \(v\). The best that measuring and phoning can do, averaged over all messages, is \(2/3\). Teleportation beats it only when \(v>1/3\), exactly where the noisy pair stops being entangled.

It needs only NumPy and Matplotlib.

      What is being computed?
      • States and gates: eight complex amplitudes, row \(4q_1+2q_2+q_3\), with qubit 1 written first. Gates are applied exactly: X, H and Z on one qubit; CNOT, Toffoli and Fredkin as reorderings of the rows; CCZ as a sign on \(|111\rangle\).
      • The wiring picture: each line joins an input row to an output row where the gate's matrix element is not zero. Its colour is the element's phase, and dashed lines carry a minus sign. Dots show the state before and after the gate: size for probability, colour for phase.
      • Arrows of single qubits: the Bloch vector of each qubit's reduced density matrix. Length 1 means that qubit has a definite state on its own; shorter means it is entangled with the others.
      • Shots: outcomes are drawn from \(|c_{abc}|^2\) with the browser's random number generator.
      • Teleportation: the standard circuit (H and CNOT make the pair, then CNOT 1→2, H on 1, measure 1 and 2, then Bob applies \(X^{m_2}\) and \(Z^{m_1}\)). A noisy pair is \(v|\Phi^+\rangle\langle\Phi^+|+(1-v)I/4\), handled exactly as a mixture of \(|\Phi^+\rangle\) and the pair with X, Y or Z applied to Bob's half. Bob's check measures his qubit along the direction of \(|\psi\rangle\); it passes with probability equal to the fidelity.
      • Checks: every gate matrix, the reduced arrows and every teleportation step agree with an independent NumPy calculation (Kronecker products and full density matrices) to about \(10^{-15}\).
