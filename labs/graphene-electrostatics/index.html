Graphene Electrostatics and Quantum Capacitance

How many electrons does graphene hold, and how many can a gate put into it? Count the states in k-space one by one, see temperature create electrons and holes, then build a graphene capacitor. Because graphene has so few states near the Dirac point, part of every gate voltage goes into raising its Fermi level: a quantum capacitance in series with the oxide.

Two cones, and a Fermi level to fill them

Near the corners of its Brillouin zone graphene's bands are cones, \(E=\pm\hbar v_F|\mathbf k|\), with \(v_F\approx10^6\) m/s. Counting the states inside a circle of radius \(k_F\), with four states (two spins, two valleys) per allowed \(\mathbf k\), gives the density of states and the carrier densities:

$$D(E)=\frac{2|E|}{\pi\hbar^2v_F^2},\qquad n=\int_0^\infty f(E)D(E)\,dE,\qquad p=\int_{-\infty}^0\big[1-f(E)\big]D(E)\,dE,\qquad f(E)=\frac{1}{1+e^{(E-E_F)/k_BT}}$$

A gate at voltage \(V\), separated from the graphene by an oxide of capacitance \(C_{ox}\) per unit area, holds a charge \(e(n-p)\). Graphene is not a metal: to hold that charge its Fermi level must move, and that costs part of the gate voltage:

$$e(n-p)=C_{ox}\left(V-\frac{E_F}{e}\right),\qquad \frac{1}{C_{tot}}=\frac{1}{C_{ox}}+\frac{1}{C_{qm}},\qquad C_{qm}=e^2\frac{d(n-p)}{dE_F}$$

One state per square of k-space.

In a sheet of side \(L\) with periodic boundaries, the wavefunction \(e^{i(k_xx+k_yy)}\) must fit a whole number of times: \(k_{x,y}=2\pi m_{x,y}/L\). Each allowed \(\mathbf k\) owns a square of area \(4\pi^2/L^2\), and holds four electrons (two spins, two valleys). Filling every state inside the Fermi circle gives the electron density. Count the dots, and compare with the formula as the sheet grows.

$$N=g\,\frac{\pi k_F^2}{4\pi^2/L^2},\qquad n=\frac{N}{L^2}=\frac{k_F^2}{\pi}=\frac{E_F^2}{\pi\hbar^2v_F^2},\qquad g=4$$

Heat makes electrons and holes in pairs.

At zero temperature the cone is filled exactly up to \(E_F\): electrons above the Dirac point if \(E_F\gt0\), holes below it if \(E_F\lt0\). At a finite temperature the Fermi function smears the edge, and since there is no gap, electrons are lifted from the valence band into the conduction band even in neutral graphene. The Sommerfeld expansion gives a simple formula; it turns out to be exactly the total, \(n+p\).

$$n+p=\frac{E_F^2}{\pi\hbar^2v_F^2}+\frac{\pi(k_BT)^2}{3\hbar^2v_F^2}\quad\text{(exact)},\qquad n=p=\frac{\pi(k_BT)^2}{6\hbar^2v_F^2}\ \ \text{at }E_F=0$$

Two capacitors in series.

For a metal plate \(Q=C_{ox}V\). Graphene has few states near the Dirac point, so adding charge raises its Fermi level, and that potential is taken from the gate voltage. The graphene behaves as a second capacitor, the quantum capacitance \(C_{qm}=e^2D(E_F)\), in series with the oxide. It matters when it is not much larger than \(C_{ox}\): for thin oxides, and near the Dirac point, where it is smallest.

$$C_{qm}=e^2\frac{2k_BT}{\pi\hbar^2v_F^2}\ln\!\left[2+2\cosh\!\left(\frac{E_F}{k_BT}\right)\right]\ \xrightarrow{\ T\to0\ }\ \frac{2e^2|E_F|}{\pi\hbar^2v_F^2},\qquad C_{ox}=\frac{\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r}{d}$$

      Step 2 · Predict, then test

      Predict first

      Load each example, make a prediction, then check it in the lab.

      Question 1 · Counting electrons

      A 100 nm × 100 nm sheet has its Fermi level 0.1 eV above the Dirac point. How many conduction electrons does it hold?

      Show answer

      About 74 by the formula: \(k_F=E_F/\hbar v_F=0.152\) nm⁻¹, so the circle holds \(\pi k_F^2/(2\pi/L)^2=18.4\) k-points, each with four states, giving \(k_F^2L^2/\pi=73.5\) electrons. Counting the grid points one by one gives 21 × 4 = 84: with so few points the circle\'s jagged edge matters. The density is \(n=k_F^2/\pi=7.3\times10^{11}\) cm⁻², about 2 electrons for every 10,000 carbon atoms.

      Question 2 · Counting electrons

      Double the Fermi energy, from 0.1 to 0.2 eV. What happens to the electron density?

      Show answer

      By the formula it goes up four times, to \(2.9\times10^{12}\) cm⁻²: \(k_F\) doubles, and the area of the Fermi circle goes as \(k_F^2\). In the 100 nm sheet the count goes from 84 to 276 electrons, a factor of 3.3, because the grid of k-points is still coarse; make the sheet larger and the factor approaches 4. Put another way, the density of states grows linearly with energy, so \(n=\int_0^{E_F}D\,dE\propto E_F^2\). For an ordinary 2D metal with a parabolic band, \(D\) is constant and \(n\) would only double.

      Question 3 · Electrons, holes and heat

      Lower the Fermi level to −0.37 eV at zero temperature. How many holes is that, per carbon atom?

      Show answer

      \(p=E_F^2/\pi\hbar^2v_F^2=1.0\times10^{13}\) cm⁻². Graphene has \(3.8\times10^{15}\) carbon atoms per cm², so that is about 2.6 holes per 1000 atoms (the video rounds it to 3). Even heavily doped graphene is changed only very slightly.

      Question 4 · Electrons, holes and heat

      Neutral graphene, \(E_F=0\): how many electrons are in the conduction band at 0 K, at 300 K and at 1000 K?

      Show answer

      None at 0 K. At a finite temperature electrons are lifted across the Dirac point, leaving an equal number of holes behind: \(n=p=\pi(k_BT)^2/6\hbar^2v_F^2\), which is \(8.1\times10^{10}\) cm⁻² at 300 K and \(9.0\times10^{11}\) cm⁻² at 1000 K. It grows as \(T^2\) because there is no gap, unlike a semiconductor, where it grows as \(e^{-E_g/2k_BT}\).

      Question 5 · Electrons, holes and heat

      The Sommerfeld expansion gives \(n\approx E_F^2/\pi\hbar^2v_F^2+\pi(k_BT)^2/3\hbar^2v_F^2\), derived for \(E_F\gg k_BT\). What does it give at \(E_F=0\), compared with the exact \(n\)?

      Show answer

      Twice the exact value: \(\pi(k_BT)^2/3\hbar^2v_F^2\) against \(\pi(k_BT)^2/6\hbar^2v_F^2\). In fact the formula is exactly \(n+p\) at every \(E_F\) and \(T\), because the Fermi–Dirac integral obeys \(F_1(x)+F_1(-x)=\pi^2/6+x^2/2\). When \(E_F\gg k_BT\), \(p\) is negligible and it becomes \(n\), as in the video.

      Question 6 · Gating

      A classic graphene device: 300 nm of SiO\(_2\) and 10 V on the back gate. How many electrons, and how much of the 10 V goes into the Fermi level?

      Show answer

      \(C_{ox}=\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r/d=11.5\) nF/cm², so a metal plate would hold \(C_{ox}V/e=7.2\times10^{11}\) cm⁻²; graphene holds \(7.1\times10^{11}\) cm⁻², and \(E_F\approx0.09\) eV (0.087 eV at 300 K, where heat supplies part of the charge; 0.098 eV at 0 K). Only about 1% of the voltage moves the Fermi level; the other 99% drops across the oxide. With such a thick oxide graphene behaves like a metal plate, \(Q\approx C_{ox}V\).

      Question 7 · Gating

      Now a thin top gate: 3 nm of SiO\(_2\) at 1 V. If graphene were a metal it would hold \(7.2\times10^{12}\) electrons per cm². How many does it actually hold?

      Show answer

      About \(5.3\times10^{12}\) cm⁻² at 0 K, 73% of the metal value. To hold that charge the Fermi level must rise to 0.27 eV, and that 0.27 V is taken from the 1 V on the gate. The oxide capacitance (1.15 µF/cm²) is no longer small next to graphene\'s quantum capacitance (6.3 µF/cm² at this Fermi level, and much less nearer the Dirac point), so the two in series give 0.97 µF/cm², 85% of \(C_{ox}\).

      Question 8 · Gating

      Where is the quantum capacitance smallest, and how small? What does it do to the total capacitance of the 3 nm gate?

      Show answer

      At the Dirac point, where there are fewest states. At 0 K it vanishes there, and so does the total. At 300 K thermal smearing gives \(C_{qm}=e^2(2k_BT\ln4)/\pi\hbar^2v_F^2=0.84\) µF/cm², and in series with \(C_{ox}=1.15\) µF/cm² the total is 0.49 µF/cm², only 42% of \(C_{ox}\). This dip in the measured capacitance near the Dirac point is how graphene's quantum capacitance is seen in experiments.

      Run the same calculation in Python

      The companion Jupyter notebook counts k-points to build the density of states, computes the electron and hole densities at any temperature (and shows that the Sommerfeld formula is exactly \(n+p\)), solves the gate equation for the Fermi level, and derives the quantum capacitance, checking each against the video's formulas. It needs NumPy, SciPy and Matplotlib.

      Download notebook .ipynb · 15 KB
      What is being computed?
      • Bands: the Dirac cones only, \(E=\pm\hbar v_F|\mathbf k|\) with \(v_F=10^6\) m/s as in the video (adjustable), and four states per \(\mathbf k\) (spin and valley). This is accurate for \(|E_F|\) up to a few tenths of an eV; the graphene band-structure lab shows where the cones bend.
      • Counting: every lattice point \(\mathbf k=\tfrac{2\pi}{L}(m_x,m_y)\) with \(|\mathbf k|\lt k_F\) is counted one by one, and compared with \(\pi k_F^2/(2\pi/L)^2\).
      • Densities: exact at every temperature: \(n=\frac{2(k_BT)^2}{\pi\hbar^2v_F^2}F_1(E_F/k_BT)\) and \(p\) with \(-E_F\), where \(F_1(x)=-\mathrm{Li}_2(-e^x)\) is the complete Fermi–Dirac integral, computed from the dilogarithm. The identity \(F_1(x)+F_1(-x)=\pi^2/6+x^2/2\) is what makes the Sommerfeld formula exact for \(n+p\).
      • Gate: \(e(n-p)=C_{ox}(V-E_F/e)\) is solved for \(E_F\) by bisection (its left side rises with \(E_F\), its right side falls, so there is exactly one solution). The graphene is grounded (held at 0 V); work-function differences and charged impurities, which shift the Dirac point in real devices, are left out.
      • Capacitances are per unit area: 1 µF/cm² = 0.01 F/m². \(C_{ox}=\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r/d\); \(C_{qm}=e^2\,d(n-p)/dE_F\) in closed form; and \(C_{tot}=e\,d(n-p)/dV\), which equals \(C_{ox}\) and \(C_{qm}\) in series.
      • Checks: the lab's dilogarithm, densities and quantum capacitance were checked against direct numerical integration with SciPy, to better than \(10^{-9}\). The notebook repeats the whole calculation independently in Python and asserts every number quoted in the answers.
