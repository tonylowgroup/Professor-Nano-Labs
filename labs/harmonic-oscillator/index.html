Quantum Harmonic Oscillator: Ladders and Coherent States

A mass on a spring, quantum style. Climb the energy ladder one rung at a time with the raising and lowering operators, see why a single rung never moves but two together do, then build the coherent state that swings back and forth like a classical pendulum.

A mass on a spring

A particle of mass \(m\) in the potential \(\tfrac12 m\omega^2x^2\). Its natural length is \(x_0=\sqrt{\hbar/m\omega}\) and its natural energy \(\hbar\omega\); the lab measures position in \(x_0\), energy in \(\hbar\omega\) and time in periods \(T=2\pi/\omega\). The lowering operator \(a\) and raising operator \(a^\dagger\) are combinations of position and momentum, and the whole Hamiltonian is built from them:

$$H=\frac{p^2}{2m}+\frac12 m\omega^2x^2=\hbar\omega\Big(a^\dagger a+\frac12\Big),\qquad a=\frac1{\sqrt2}\Big(\frac{x}{x_0}+\frac{i\,x_0\,p}{\hbar}\Big),\qquad [a,a^\dagger]=1$$

For a \(^{40}\mathrm{Ca}^+\) ion vibrating at \(\omega/2\pi=1\) MHz, as in the trapped-ion lab, \(x_0\approx16\) nm and \(\hbar\omega\approx4\) neV; each rung of the ladder is one phonon.

Every rung is one ħω above the last.

The raising operator \(a^\dagger\) turns \(|n\rangle\) into \(\sqrt{n+1}\,|n{+}1\rangle\); the lowering operator \(a\) turns it into \(\sqrt n\,|n{-}1\rangle\). On the ground state, \(a\) gives zero: there is no rung below, so the lowest energy is \(\hbar\omega/2\), not zero. Written with a derivative, \(a^\dagger\) turns each wavefunction into the next one up, with one more node.

$$a^\dagger|n\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}\,|n{+}1\rangle,\qquad a|n\rangle=\sqrt n\,|n{-}1\rangle,\qquad a|0\rangle=0,\qquad E_n=\Big(n+\frac12\Big)\hbar\omega,\qquad a^\dagger=\frac1{\sqrt2}\Big(\xi-\frac{d}{d\xi}\Big),\ \ \xi=\frac x{x_0}$$

A single rung never moves; two rungs do.

An eigenstate only picks up a phase, \(e^{-iE_nt/\hbar}\), so its probability density is frozen in time. Put two rungs together and their phases turn at different rates: the density sloshes at their difference frequency. Position links only neighbouring rungs, so the centre swings only when the two rungs are next to each other.

$$|\psi(t)\rangle=c_m e^{-i(m+\frac12)\omega t}|m\rangle+c_n e^{-i(n+\frac12)\omega t}|n\rangle,\qquad |\psi(x,t)|^2\ \text{beats at}\ (n-m)\,\omega,\qquad \langle x\rangle\neq0\ \text{only if}\ |n-m|=1$$

The quantum state that swings like a pendulum.

A coherent state \(|\alpha\rangle\) is the ground-state Gaussian moved off centre. Each of its amplitudes turns at its own rate, yet together they carry the Gaussian back and forth along the classical path without changing its shape. It is the one kind of state the lowering operator leaves unchanged. Squeeze its width and it breathes as it swings.

$$|\alpha\rangle=e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\sum_n\frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}|n\rangle,\qquad a|\alpha\rangle=\alpha|\alpha\rangle,\qquad \langle x\rangle=\sqrt2\,|\alpha|\,x_0\cos(\omega t-\varphi),\qquad P(n)=e^{-|\alpha|^2}\frac{|\alpha|^{2n}}{n!}$$

        Step 2 · Predict, then test

        Predict first

        Load each example, make a prediction, then check it in the lab.

        Question 1 · The ladder

        Apply the lowering operator \(a\) to the ground state. What do you get? So what is the lowest energy the oscillator can have?

        Show answer

        Zero: \(a|0\rangle=0\), the ladder has no rung below. The lowest energy is \(\hbar\omega/2\), the zero-point energy, and the ground state has the smallest uncertainty allowed, \(\Delta x\,\Delta p=\hbar/2\). Even at absolute zero the particle keeps jiggling.

        Question 2 · The ladder

        Apply \(a^\dagger\) to \(|3\rangle\). What number multiplies the new state, and how many nodes does its wavefunction have?

        Show answer

        \(a^\dagger|3\rangle=\sqrt4\,|4\rangle=2|4\rangle\), with energy \(4.5\,\hbar\omega\). The wavefunction \(\psi_4\) has four nodes: each step up adds one. Plot A shows the same step done with the derivative form, \((\xi-d/d\xi)\psi_3/\sqrt2\), landing on top of \(2\psi_4\).

        Question 3 · The ladder

        At rung 30, where is the particle most likely to be found? Compare with a classical mass on a spring with the same energy.

        Show answer

        Near the turning points, \(\pm\sqrt{61}\,x_0\approx\pm7.8\,x_0\), just as for the classical mass, which moves slowest there. The quantum density wiggles around the classical curve \(1/\pi\sqrt{A^2-x^2}\), and for high rungs the two agree on average: the correspondence principle. At low rungs they look nothing alike; the ground state is most likely found in the centre.

        Question 4 · Superpositions

        A single rung, \(|2\rangle\). Press play. What changes in time, and what does not?

        Show answer

        Only the phase of the amplitude turns, once every \(2T/5\), as its arrow in plot A shows. The probability density does not change at all. That is why eigenstates are called stationary states.

        Question 5 · Superpositions

        An equal mix of \(|0\rangle\) and \(|1\rangle\). How often does the density swing back and forth, and how far does its centre move?

        Show answer

        Once per period \(T\), at the frequency \(\omega\) of the energy difference, \(E_1-E_0=\hbar\omega\). The centre swings as \(\langle x\rangle=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\,x_0\cos\omega t\approx0.71\,x_0\cos\omega t\). The relative phase φ only shifts when the swing starts.

        Question 6 · Superpositions

        An equal mix of \(|0\rangle\) and \(|2\rangle\). Does the centre move?

        Show answer

        No, \(\langle x\rangle=0\) at all times: position only links neighbouring rungs, and 0 and 2 are not neighbours. Instead the packet breathes, twice per period: its width swings between about \(0.89\,x_0\) and \(1.49\,x_0\).

        Question 7 · Coherent states

        Pull the ground state aside to \(2\sqrt2\,x_0\) and let go. Does it spread out, like the free Gaussian packet in the wave packet lab? How many quanta does it hold?

        Show answer

        It never spreads. It swings between \(\pm2.83\,x_0\) once per period, keeping its width of \(0.71\,x_0\) and \(\Delta x\,\Delta p=\hbar/2\) at every moment, exactly following the classical mass. It holds \(|\alpha|^2=4\) quanta on average, in a Poisson spread: \(P(n)\) is largest at \(n=3\) and \(n=4\), about 19.5% each. The spring's pull is what stops the spreading that a free packet suffers.

        Question 8 · Coherent states

        Squeeze the starting width to half the ground state's. What happens to the width as it swings? Is \(\Delta x\,\Delta p\) still \(\hbar/2\)?

        Show answer

        The width breathes twice per period, from \(0.35\,x_0\) out to \(1.41\,x_0\) and back. \(\Delta x\,\Delta p=\hbar/2\) only at the narrowest and widest moments; in between it is larger, up to about \(1.06\,\hbar\), because position and momentum become correlated. The lowering operator no longer leaves the state unchanged: only true coherent states have that property.

        What is being computed?
        • Eigenstates: \(\psi_n(\xi)=H_n(\xi)e^{-\xi^2/2}/\sqrt{2^nn!\sqrt\pi}\), built with the three-term recurrence \(\psi_{n+1}=\sqrt{2/(n+1)}\,\xi\psi_n-\sqrt{n/(n+1)}\,\psi_{n-1}\), which stays accurate up to the 90 rungs used here.
        • Ladder operators: on the amplitudes, \(a\) and \(a^\dagger\) shift each \(c_n\) by one rung with the factors \(\sqrt n\) and \(\sqrt{n+1}\). Plot A on the first tab applies \(a^\dagger=(\xi-d/d\xi)/\sqrt2\) directly to the wavefunction, with a finite-difference derivative.
        • Time evolution: each amplitude turns as \(e^{-i(n+1/2)\omega t}\); nothing else changes. Averages come from the ladder operators: \(\langle x\rangle=\sqrt2\,\mathrm{Re}\langle a\rangle\), \(\langle p\rangle=\sqrt2\,\mathrm{Im}\langle a\rangle\), and the spreads from \(\langle a^2\rangle\) and \(\langle a^\dagger a\rangle\).
        • Coherent and squeezed states: coherent amplitudes come from the formula. A squeezed state is a Gaussian of a different width, projected onto the first 90 eigenstates; its norm is 1 to better than \(10^{-12}\). The overlap with \(a|\psi\rangle\) is \(|\langle a\rangle|^2/\langle a^\dagger a\rangle\), which is 1 exactly for coherent states.
        • Classical particle: at the same energy, \(x(t)=A\cos(\omega t-\varphi)\), and its probability density is \(1/\pi\sqrt{A^2-x^2}\) between the turning points \(\pm A\).
        • Checks: the eigenfunctions agree with SciPy's Hermite polynomials to about \(10^{-14}\); coherent states match the moving Gaussian, and squeezed states match the analytic breathing width, to about \(10^{-15}\).
