From Atoms to a Band

Line atoms up in a chain and switch on the coupling between neighbours. One level per atom spreads into an energy band, and counting those levels gives the density of states.

One orbital per atom, one coupling between neighbours

Each site in the chain contributes one atomic orbital with energy \(\varepsilon\). Neighbouring orbitals are coupled by a hopping energy \(t<0\), and the ends are open. The Hamiltonian is tridiagonal, and its \(N\) levels are known exactly:

$$H=\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon&t&&\\ t&\varepsilon&\ddots&\\ &\ddots&\ddots&t\\ &&t&\varepsilon\end{pmatrix},\qquad E_n=\varepsilon+2t\cos\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{N+1}\right),\quad n=1,\dots,N.$$

Energies are in eV and lengths in Å. One spin channel is counted throughout.

Add atoms. Watch an energy band form.

Each site contributes one state. Turn on the coupling and increase N to see those states split into a densely filled band.

$$W_N=E_N-E_1=4|t|\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi}{N+1}\right)\;\longrightarrow\;W_\infty=4|t|\quad\text{as } N\to\infty$$

Pull the atoms apart. Collapse the band.

Keep the number of orbitals fixed and vary only the spacing, which sets the coupling. The fan follows every level as the spacing changes; the vertical line marks the current spacing.

$$t(a)=t_{\rm ref}\,\frac{a}{a_{\rm ref}}\,e^{-\kappa(a-a_{\rm ref})},\qquad W_\infty(a)=4\,|t(a)|$$

Count states. Build the density of states.

The shaded energy window is linked across all three plots. Move it from the band centre to an edge, or animate N to turn separate peaks into a smooth curve.

$$\Omega_N(E)=\#\{E_n\le E\},\qquad D_\sigma(E)=\frac{1}{Na}\sum_n G_\sigma(E-E_n),\qquad D_\infty(E)=\frac{1}{\pi a\sqrt{4t^2-(E-\varepsilon)^2}}$$

      Step 2 · Predict, then test

      Predict first

      Load each example, make a prediction, then press Play or move the slider to check it.

      Question 1 · Add atoms

      With two atoms, |t| = 0.4 eV and ε = 0, what are the two energies? If you keep doubling N, does the band keep getting wider?

      Show answer

      The two levels are \(\varepsilon\pm t = \pm 0.4\) eV: a bonding and an antibonding state. The band does not keep widening. The full width is \(W_N = 4|t|\cos[\pi/(N+1)]\): 0.80, 1.29, 1.50 and 1.57 eV for N = 2, 4, 8 and 16, approaching \(4|t| = 1.6\) eV. More atoms fill in the band; they do not stretch it.

      Question 2 · Add atoms

      Switch the coupling off completely with eight atoms. How many states are there, and at what energy?

      Show answer

      Still eight states, all at \(E=\varepsilon\). Isolated atoms have identical, degenerate levels; the coupling is what splits them into a band. Raise |t| slowly and watch the eight levels fan out symmetrically around ε.

      Question 3 · Spacing

      Pull the atoms from 5 Å to 7 Å apart. Do any states disappear?

      Show answer

      No: there are still N states. With κ = 1 Å⁻¹ the coupling falls from 0.40 to about 0.076 eV, so the band narrows from 1.6 eV to about 0.30 eV and the levels crowd toward ε, the isolated-atom limit. Squeeze them to 3 Å instead and |t| rises to about 1.77 eV, a band about 7.1 eV wide.

      Question 4 · Density of states

      A window 0.16 eV wide sits at the centre of the band. Will the same window hold more states near the band edge or fewer?

      Show answer

      More. With N = 64 the window holds 4 states at the centre but 13 states when centred at −0.72 eV, near the bottom edge (window centre −0.90). Near an edge \(E_n\) changes slowly with n, so many levels share a small energy range. In the large-N limit \(D_\infty(E)\) is smallest at the centre and diverges at both edges, a van Hove singularity.

      Question 5 · Density of states

      Change the display resolution σ/|t| from 0.08 to 0.25. Does the number of states in the window change?

      Show answer

      No. The count comes from the exact levels, and σ only sets how wide each drawn peak is. Larger σ blurs the separate peaks into one smooth curve, but the area check stays at 1: each level still contributes exactly one state.

      Question 6 · Density of states

      Keep σ fixed and raise N from 8 to 256. Which curve does the finite-N density of states approach?

      Show answer

      The bulk curve with the same broadening (dotted), not the ideal bulk curve (dashed). The ideal curve diverges at the edges; any finite resolution rounds those spikes off. The two bulk curves only meet when σ goes to zero as well.

      What is being computed?
      • Levels: the exact eigenvalues of the open, real, tridiagonal chain, \(E_n=\varepsilon+2t\cos[n\pi/(N+1)]\). With \(t<0\), \(n=1\) is the lowest level. The finite width is \(W_N=4|t|\cos[\pi/(N+1)]\), and the large-N width is \(W_\infty=4|t|\). The notebook diagonalizes the matrix independently with NumPy and agrees to about \(10^{-15}\) eV.
      • Spacing: the hopping follows the approximate dependence \(t\propto a\,e^{-\kappa a}\), normalized to \(t_{\rm ref}=-0.40\) eV at \(a_{\rm ref}=5\) Å. These are illustrative inputs, not a fit to a particular material. Only nearest neighbours are coupled.
      • Density of states: a finite chain has a DOS made of delta functions, \(D_N(E)=\frac{1}{Na}\sum_n\delta(E-E_n)\). Each one is drawn as a unit-area Gaussian of width \(\sigma\), so \(\int D_\sigma\,dE=1/a\) per length and 1 per site. \(\sigma\) is a display resolution, not a temperature or a lifetime.
      • Bulk references: the ideal curve is \(D_\infty(E)=1/[\pi a\sqrt{4t^2-(E-\varepsilon)^2}]\), clipped where it diverges at the edges. The broadened bulk curve applies the same \(\sigma\) to the continuum, evaluated by midpoint quadrature over 1024 values of \(q=n\pi/(N+1)\).
