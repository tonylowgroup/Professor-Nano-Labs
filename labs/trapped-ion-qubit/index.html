Trapped Ions and Phonon Sidebands

A calcium ion stores a qubit in two of its levels, and the whole ion chain shares its motion as phonons. Tune the laser to the carrier or to a sideband, pass a qubit through the phonon bus, cool the chain to its motional ground state, then read the qubit out.

An optical qubit that shares a phonon bus

As in the video, the qubit is a calcium ion, Ca⁺. Its ground state 4S1/2 is \(|0\rangle\) and the long-lived excited state 3D5/2 is \(|1\rangle\), joined by a 729 nm transition that is dipole-forbidden and lives for about a second: an optical qubit. Inside the trap the video writes the same two levels as \(|g\rangle\) and \(|e\rangle\). The ions in a Paul trap also share their motion, quantized into phonons \(|n_p\rangle\) spaced by \(\hbar\omega_p\), so each ion's states form a ladder \(|g,n_p\rangle\), \(|e,n_p\rangle\). The laser's colour decides which rungs it connects:

$$\underbrace{|g,n_p\rangle\leftrightarrow|e,n_p\rangle}_{\text{carrier: }\hbar\omega_0}\qquad\underbrace{|g,n_p\rangle\leftrightarrow|e,n_p+1\rangle}_{\text{blue sideband: }\hbar(\omega_0+\omega_p)}\qquad\underbrace{|g,n_p\rangle\leftrightarrow|e,n_p-1\rangle}_{\text{red sideband: }\hbar(\omega_0-\omega_p)}$$

One thing the video does not need, but a simulation does, is how fast each transition goes. The carrier flips the ion at the Rabi frequency \(\Omega\); a sideband also has to change the motion, so it is slower, at \(\eta\sqrt{n_p+1}\,\Omega\) (blue) or \(\eta\sqrt{n_p}\,\Omega\) (red). Here \(\Omega/2\pi=50\) kHz, so a carrier π pulse takes 10 µs, and the Lamb–Dicke parameter \(\eta=0.1\), so a sideband π pulse takes about 100 µs.

In the video: Chapter 2, computation with trapped-ion qubits

The laser's colour picks the transition.

The green carrier changes only the electronic state, so the phonon number is conserved. Raise the laser frequency by one vibrational quantum and the blue sideband excites the ion while adding a phonon; lower it and the red sideband excites the ion while taking one away. Start from \(|g,0_p\rangle\) and see which colours can do anything at all.

In the video: Chapter 2, phonon-mediated coupling

The phonon bus lets qubit 2 talk to qubit 3.

The shared motion acts as a bus qubit, \(|0_p\rangle\) or \(|1_p\rangle\). When the bus is in \(|0_p\rangle\), the red laser lowers a qubit and the blue laser raises it; when it is in \(|1_p\rangle\), their roles swap. Apply pulses to qubit 2 and qubit 3 and use that rule to pass a qubit along the chain, or to entangle two ions that never touch.

In the video: Chapter 3, sideband cooling of qubits

Pump the phonons out, one at a time.

After ordinary laser cooling the chain still holds a few phonons. A red-sideband pulse moves \(|g,n_p\rangle\) to \(|e,n_p-1\rangle\), taking a phonon away; the ion then emits a photon and drops back to \(|g\rangle\), usually keeping the lower phonon number. Repeat, and the ion piles up in its motional ground state \(|g,0_p\rangle\).

In the video: Chapter 4, qubit encoding, initialization and readout

Reset with optical pumping, read out with fluorescence.

Optical pumping empties \(|1\rangle\) through the short-lived 4P level, which decays to \(|0\rangle\). A 729 nm pulse then writes the qubit. To read it, a laser on the 4S–4P transition makes the ion glow only if it is in \(|0\rangle\): bright means 0, dark means 1. A superposition gives bright on some shots and dark on others.

      Step 2 · Predict, then test

      Predict first

      Load each example, make a prediction, then press Play, add a pulse or take shots to check it.

      Question 1 · Sidebands

      The ion is cold, in \(|g,0_p\rangle\). Which of the three laser colours can excite it, and which cannot?

      Show answer

      The carrier and the blue sideband can; the red sideband cannot. Red has to take a phonon away, and in \(|0_p\rangle\) there is none to take: there is no \(|e,-1_p\rangle\). That missing red sideband is how experiments confirm the ion really is in its motional ground state.

      Question 2 · Sidebands

      A carrier π pulse takes 10 µs. How long does a blue-sideband π pulse take from \(|g,0_p\rangle\)? And from \(|g,1_p\rangle\)?

      Show answer

      100 µs from \(|g,0_p\rangle\), ten times longer, because the sideband runs at \(\eta\Omega\) with \(\eta=0.1\). From \(|g,1_p\rangle\) it is faster, 71 µs, because the rate grows as \(\sqrt{n_p+1}\): more phonons, faster sidebands.

      Question 3 · Sidebands

      The ion is warm: a thermal mix with an average of one phonon. Drive the blue sideband. Will it ever be fully excited?

      Show answer

      No. Each phonon number flops at its own rate \(\eta\sqrt{n_p+1}\,\Omega\), so the oscillations drift out of step and wash out. The best you get is about 0.82, at 72 µs. The shape of this washed-out flopping is a thermometer for the ion's motion.

      Question 4 · Phonon bus

      Qubit 3 is excited and the bus is in \(|0_p\rangle\). What does a red π pulse on qubit 3 do? Now start from \(|g_2 g_3, 1_p\rangle\) instead: what does the same red pulse do?

      Show answer

      With the bus in \(|0_p\rangle\), red lowers qubit 3 to \(|g_3\rangle\) and puts a phonon on the bus: \(|e_3,0_p\rangle\to|g_3,1_p\rangle\). With the bus in \(|1_p\rangle\), red raises it instead, \(|g_3,1_p\rangle\to|e_3,0_p\rangle\), and empties the bus. Which way a qubit goes depends on the bus: exactly the rule the video uses for qubits to communicate.

      Question 5 · Phonon bus

      A carrier π pulse excited qubit 2, then a red π/2 pulse left it half in \(|e_2,0_p\rangle\) and half in \(|g_2,1_p\rangle\). Predict what a red π pulse on qubit 3 does, then add it.

      Show answer

      Where the bus holds a phonon, red raises qubit 3 and takes the phonon back; where it holds none, nothing happens. The result is \((|e_2 g_3\rangle-|g_2 e_3\rangle)/\sqrt2\) with the bus back in \(|0_p\rangle\): qubits 2 and 3 are fully entangled (concurrence 1), though no laser ever touched both. This is the idea behind the entangling gates the video mentions.

      Question 6 · Phonon bus

      Run the same three pulses, but with the bus starting in \(|1_p\rangle\), a chain that was not cooled. Do the qubits still end up entangled?

      Show answer

      No. The bus ends up spread over \(|0_p\rangle\), \(|1_p\rangle\) and \(|2_p\rangle\) and stays entangled with the qubits, and the red pulses no longer have the right length because the rate depends on \(\sqrt{n_p}\). Extra motion ruins the gate, which is why the video cools the chain first.

      Question 7 · Sideband cooling

      Each cycle uses a red pulse that is exactly a π pulse for \(|g,1_p\rangle\to|e,0_p\rangle\). After 40 cycles, does the whole population reach \(|g,0_p\rangle\)?

      Show answer

      No, only about half. The red rate grows as \(\sqrt{n_p}\), so for \(n_p=4\) the same pulse is a 2π pulse: the ion goes up and comes straight back, and the phonon stays. About 40% of the population piles up in \(4_p\) (and a little in \(16_p\)). Switch to varied pulse lengths and about 97% reaches \(0_p\) in the same 40 cycles, over 99% in 80.

      Question 8 · Readout

      The qubit is in an equal superposition and the detector sees only 3 photons from a bright ion, with a threshold of 2. Out of 1000 shots, how many will be called bright, and how often is a bright ion mistaken for a dark one?

      Show answer

      About 440, not 500. A bright ion gives fewer than 2 counts about 16% of the time, and a dark ion more than 1 about 4% of the time, so the bright fraction comes out near \(0.5\times0.84+0.5\times0.04=0.44\). Brighten the detector to 8 photons with a threshold of 3 and both errors fall below 2%: the nearly perfect bright-or-dark readout of the video.

      What is being computed?
      • Notation: as in the video, \(|g\rangle=|0\rangle\) is 4S1/2 and \(|e\rangle=|1\rangle\) is 3D5/2 of Ca⁺; \(|n_p\rangle\) is the number of phonons in one shared motional mode of frequency \(\omega_p\); the carrier is at \(\omega_0\) and the sidebands at \(\omega_0\pm\omega_p\). The colours (green carrier, blue and red sidebands) match the video's diagrams.
      • Transitions: in the Lamb–Dicke limit (\(\eta\ll1\)) and with resolved sidebands (\(\Omega\ll\omega_p\)), each laser colour drives only its own transitions, and each \(|g,n_p\rangle\leftrightarrow|e,n_p'\rangle\) pair is an exact two-level rotation at \(\Omega\), \(\eta\sqrt{n_p+1}\,\Omega\) or \(\eta\sqrt{n_p}\,\Omega\). The notebook checks this against the full ion–phonon Hamiltonian with \(\omega_p/2\pi=1\) MHz. The carrier agrees within 0.5%. On a sideband, the off-resonant carrier shifts the line by \(\Omega^2/2\omega_p\approx2\pi\times1.25\) kHz (a light shift); retuning the laser by that amount, as experiments do, brings agreement within 1%.
      • Phonon bus: two ions and one mode, kept up to \(4_p\). Pulse areas refer to \(|g,0_p\rangle\leftrightarrow|e,1_p\rangle\) for blue and \(|g,1_p\rangle\leftrightarrow|e,0_p\rangle\) for red; other phonon numbers rotate faster by \(\sqrt{n_p+1}\) or \(\sqrt{n_p}\). Concurrence is shown when the bus holds a single phonon number.
      • Sideband cooling: a rate model on the phonon distribution, starting thermal. Each cycle moves \(|g,n_p\rangle\to|e,n_p-1\rangle\) with probability \(\sin^2(\theta\sqrt{n_p}/2)\), then spontaneous emission returns the ion to \(|g\rangle\), changing \(n_p\) by ±1 with the chosen recoil probability. "Varied" cycles through pulses tuned to \(1_p\), \(2_p\) and \(4_p\).
      • Readout: each shot projects the qubit; a bright ion gives Poisson-distributed counts with the chosen mean plus background, a dark one background only. Optical pumping returns 94% of the \(|1\rangle\) population to \(|0\rangle\) per cycle, roughly the branching of the 4P3/2 decay in Ca⁺. In Ca⁺ the readout uses the 397 nm line to 4P1/2 and the reset uses 854 nm to 4P3/2; the lab, like the video, draws one 4P level.
      • Checks: the notebook repeats every calculation in NumPy and agrees with this page to about \(10^{-15}\).
