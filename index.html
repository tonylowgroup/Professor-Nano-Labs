Labs are grouped into seven tracks, from the ideas quantum mechanics is built on, through qubits, crystals and the geometry of quantum states, to electrons moving through real nanoscale devices, the magnets that store information, and the electromagnetic fields underneath it all. How it works

Watch. Predict. Test.

Each lab is built around a short loop. The prediction step is the one that makes the physics stick.

  1. Step 1

    Watch

    Start with the Professor Nano episode. Each lab links back to the video it goes with.

  2. Step 2

    Predict

    Before you move a slider, commit to a guess. Which way will the curve shift, and by roughly how much?

  3. Step 3

    Test

    Run the live model, compare it with your prediction, then open the explanation when you're ready.

  • Live, not pre-recorded

    Every plot is computed in your browser as you move the controls. Nothing to install, and it works on a phone.

  • Code you can read

    Each lab comes with its Jupyter notebook, so you can check the physics line by line and change it.

  • Assumptions stated

    Every model says what it includes, what it leaves out, and where it stops being valid.

Professor Nano

big ideas precisely explained in nano time

Hello, I’m Tony Low, a professor at the University of Minnesota, with over two decades of research experience in semiconductors, nano-devices, quantum materials and emerging technologies at IBM T.J. Watson Research Center, Columbia University, Yale University, Purdue University and the National University of Singapore.

Professor Nano shares key ideas through concise, 10-minute “knowledge nuggets” built from first principles, spanning semiconductors, electronic and optical devices, quantum physics, nanotechnology and the science behind future computing. We won’t shy away from math, because equations often provide the clearest path to understanding.

These labs put those equations in motion: change a parameter and see what the model predicts.

Summer school

Quantum + Chips

From atoms to materials to devices to chips

Two weeks every summer · University of Minnesota · For undergraduates

Quantum + Chips is a two-week summer school at the University of Minnesota, led by Professor Tony Low with experts from industry and academia. It is open to undergraduates from a wide range of disciplines, such as physics, engineering, chemistry and mathematics, and teaches a first-principles approach that runs from atoms to materials to devices to chips: going “from quantum to chips”.

Since it began in 2023, the school has brought together about 50 students each year from across the US and abroad, typically from late July to early August. Along with lectures and a hackathon, students tour labs and companies, hear from industry, and make lasting connections with each other.

Week 1Deep dive

Lectures from atoms to devices, each paired with a hands-on Python lab, and a hackathon on challenge problems in quantum and chips. The lectures are on the Professor Nano channel.

Week 2Deep tech

Talks by experts on devices, semiconductor and quantum chips, clean-room and company visits, and a careers panel. Speakers in past years have come from Intel, Nvidia, Google, Honeywell, Quantinuum, Seagate, SkyWater, NVE and 3M.
